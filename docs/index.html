The flow

One set of vectors, from Keras to the board

HGQ2 model→da4ml RTL→Verilator, 0 mismatches→Vivado 2024.2→silicon, MISR = sim

Every stage reuses the same golden vectors. On the boards an on-chip signature (MISR) over every decision is compared with the Verilator signature, so a single wrong bit anywhere fails the run. Nothing was ever written to any board's flash: every bitstream is a volatile JTAG or fpga-manager load.

Every figure carries a label: silicon measured on the board, sim Verilator, model Vivado timing or out-of-context place and route, derived computed from silicon counts with stated model terms. Every latency states its start event, stop event, instrument and exclusions.

Artifact B · BCU-1525 · xcvu9p-fsgd2104-2L-e · 161.13 MHz GTY user clock

Surface-code decoder in a real link loop

The link was the loop. With Aurora, the round trip alone was 726–732 ns and the decoder added only 37–118 ns. A thin custom 64B/66B link on the GTYs, on one shared reference clock and one clock domain, cut the round trip to 292–298 ns and brought both loops under the published 550 ns.

The networks

  • d = 3: 24-128-64-32-1, 30,258 EBOPs. Decode 6 cycles = 37.2 ns silicon, 3.3× shorter than the published 124 ns (Kintex-7, measured).
  • d = 5: 120-192-96-96-1, 933,465 EBOPs, one syndrome per cycle. Decode 19 cycles = 117.9 ns silicon, still under the published d = 3 figure.
  • The most accurate d = 5 core (3.58 M EBOPs) does not route on the VU9P at 6.206 ns model. A register on each all-to-all layer input closed the smaller one.

Artifact C · USRP X310 · XC7K410T · 200 MHz radio clock

Waveform classifier on the live ADC stream

A quantized classifier inside an RFNoC block, between the ADC front end and the stock radio block, labels every 200 MS/s sample's 64-sample window as noise, CW, LFM or BPSK. The X310 makes its own test signals: RF A TX/RX through 30 dB of attenuation into RF A RX2, at 1 GHz, on two UBX-160 boards.

What fixed BPSK

v1 was trained through a ±20 MHz Butterworth low-pass with noise shaped by the same filter. The real path is flat to about ±80 MHz with white noise, which made rectangular BPSK look like noise. Simulating with the measured response and real noise reproduced v1's silicon numbers class by class (BPSK 65.2 against 65.1). v2 trains through the measured response and noise spectrum; same recipe, same selection rule.

Latency and proof

  • Pipeline 22 cycles = 110 ns, newest sample in to decision out sim, read back from the block.
  • Onset at the classifier's input to its first correct decision, median at SNR ≥ 10 dB: BPSK 155, LFM 175, CW 330 ns; 100 % detected silicon. About 160 ns more from the ADC input derived.
  • Bit-exact: 20 captures, 84 M decisions, MISR equal to a Verilator replay of the captured samples.
  • Event revision: up to 16 records per packet, events off at reset; 0 of 16 session starts failed; 40,567 of 40,567 dense events equal the replay.
Start pulse onset at the classifier's tap (TX schedule plus the measured 133.70-sample delay). Stop the tap time of the first decision of the pulse's class, plus 22 cycles. Instrument the X310's own radio time, carried with every decision. Excluded the ~160 ns of ADC and radio front end before the tap.
